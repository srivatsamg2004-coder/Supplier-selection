Model configuration shared

Uncertainty & simulation stochastic only

Decisions xi are fixed from the deterministic MILP (expected-value inputs); only δ and P are random. Independent draws across suppliers — no correlation is asserted (data gives no basis).

Deterministic vs stochastic

The deterministic column is a single fixed-input value. The stochastic column describes a distribution of outcomes under the configured uncertainty; they answer different questions, so compare them as “point estimate vs spread”, not as competing objective values.

Methodology

Tags: PPT from your presentation · data estimated from the dataset · assumption needs confirmation.

E[δ²] = σ² + μ²                          [PPT] μ, σ from Quality_Deviation per supplier [data] (population σ)
Δk = kᵇ − kˢ                             [PPT]  kˢ = 2.5 [data], kᵇ user-set [assumption]
C* = P + Δk · E[δ²]                      [PPT] financially explicit unit cost
min Z = Σ C*ᵢ xᵢ                         [PPT] ERP baseline: min Σ Pᵢ xᵢ (same constraints)
s.t. Σ xᵢ = D;  Σ Pᵢ xᵢ ≤ B;  xᵢ ≥ MOQᵢ yᵢ;  xᵢ ≤ capᵢ yᵢ;  yᵢ ∈ {0,1}; yᵢ = 0 if not approved
     [PPT demand/MOQ/budget/link; capacity: PPT “potentially”, value from data]

Deterministic: μ, σ, P fixed at historical estimates; MILP solved exactly (branch & bound, javascript-lp-solver). Budget uses purchase price P, not C*, as the PPT specifies.

Stochastic: for each of N runs, draw δi and Pi for every supplier (same draws applied to both plans — common random numbers), compute realised cost Σ xᵢ(Pᵢ + Δk δᵢ²). Percentiles describe the spread of simulated outcomes; the CI describes uncertainty in the estimated mean (mean ± z·s/√N). Probabilities are frequencies over N runs.

Distribution rationale: with only 20 observations per supplier, the empirical bootstrap avoids asserting a shape; Normal for δ and lognormal for P are offered as options, not claimed as fitted. Seeded generator: Mulberry32.

Limitations: synthetic data; no validation against real outcomes; supplier-side loss assumed already in price; defect-rate cost and lead time are not in the PPT objective; independence across suppliers; not two-stage stochastic programming.
